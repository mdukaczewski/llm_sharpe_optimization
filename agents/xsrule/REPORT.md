# xsrule lane — non-ML cross-sectional minute-feature books (2026-10-29)

Scripts: `agents/xsrule/xlib.py` (helpers, own per-ticker extra cache), `x1_onrule.py` / `x1b_onrule.py` / `x4_confirm.py` /
`x5_clean.py` (overnight rule signals), `x2_cc.py` (next-day close-to-close, rule + LightGBM, holds 1/2/5), `x3_cal.py`
(overnight vs intraday complement; calendar / FOMC), `port_check.py` (portfolio orientation), `final.py` (regenerates the

(264 -> 386 names); the final run used universe(1500) after `MINUTE DONE`.

## Result in one paragraph
The existing overnight rule s = 0.5*z30 - z60 improves materially when three pre-close terms are added with FIXED
weights (no per-fold or in-sample optimisation; weights were set once at the scale of the base signal and not tuned):

    cAx = 0.5*z30 - z60 - 0.5*z5l + 0.3*zon_prev - rank(vlast_x)

z5l = last-5-minute return (15:54 -> 15:59) / lagged 20d sd (late-day reversal), zon_prev = previous night's return / sd
(overnight continuation), vlast_x = cross-sectional rank (pct, demeaned) of the last-hour share of continuous-session volume,
closing auction excluded (own feature, `xlib.ticker_extra`; corr 0.96 with rlib's vlast_share). Every input is known by the
15:59 bar; fill at the official close, exit at the 09:31 bar (project convention). Long-only top-20 with cAx > 1.5 on the
150 most liquid names: full-sample 2.25 vs 2.04 for the base rule (same code, same names), fold-OOS 2.00 vs 1.83.
The L/S version (top-20 / bottom-20, no threshold) is the genuinely new object: the base rule's L/S is 1.23 (150 names) /
1.04 (386 names), the composite's L/S is 1.77-1.94 with correlation 0.22-0.31 to the existing rule sleeve, 0.30-0.34 to
the LightGBM overnight sleeve and ~0 to SPY. A variant cBx that replaces part of the z5l term by zmoc (official close vs
15:59 print, -0.3 each) is better for the broad-universe L/S (fold-OOS 1.83 vs 1.55 on 684 names) but uses the closing
print itself as a signal for a fill at that print; see the leakage section. Everything else in the brief was dead:
next-day close-to-close (rule and learned), 2/5-day holds, the intraday complement, and calendar/FOMC conditioning.

## What I would keep and why
1. `xsr_cBx_ls20_u1500` is the best new sleeve by every metric that matters here (fold-OOS 2.05, 1.94 at 1.5 bps, 0.17 to
   the portfolio, improves with breadth) IF the closing-print term is accepted (next paragraph). Its 2022H2 fold (-2.2)
   and -22.7% maxDD are the price of 14.5% vol on 1500 names with short books.
2. `xsr_cAx_ls20_u150` is the clean (fully pre-close) L/S diversifier: fold-OOS 1.65, zero market beta, low drawdown,
   but it has been flat from 2021H2 to 2024H1 and costs bite (1.26 at 1.5 bps). Keep as the conservative version of (1).
3. `xsr_cAx_lo20_s15_u150` should REPLACE the existing rule sleeve xslo20s1_150 rather than be added (0.87 correlated):
   same book, same names, three extra pre-close terms, fold-OOS 2.04 vs 1.83 and a better worst fold.
Not kept: cBx LO (duplicate), cAx L/S on 1500 (worse than on 150), all c->c / hold / complement / calendar books (below).

## Leakage and implementability
* Causality: all signal inputs end at the 15:59 bar (z30, z60 = r(329,389), z5l = r(384,389), zon_prev, vlast_x from
  bins 330..389 over 0..389). z-scores use a lagged 20-day sd (rlib.zsd, shift(1)); cross-sectional ranks use the same
  day's names only. Fill at the official close, exit at the 09:31 bar: identical to the existing rule and LightGBM books.
* The one exception is cBx's zmoc = official close / 15:59 close - 1 (rlib.ticker_frame), which is the closing-auction
  print itself. A fill "at the close" after knowing the closing print is only realisable with an order that trades at
  the auction price (MOC orders must be entered by 15:50 NYSE / 15:55 Nasdaq) using the published auction imbalance as a
  proxy for zmoc. The clean composite cAx exists for exactly this reason; on the 150-name LO book zmoc adds nothing
  (1.99 vs 2.04), on the L/S books it adds 0.1 (150 names) to 0.5 (1500 names) of fold-OOS. The same caveat applies in
  weaker form to any book that uses rlib's close-based features (zoc, zvwap, rngpos, vlast_share includes the auction
  volume), which is why vlast_x excludes bin 390.
* Universe: ranks by today's median dollar volume (survivorship, same as every other sleeve). The LO book uses the top
  150, chosen earlier in the project, not here; the composite weights were fixed a priori (0.5 / 0.3 / 1.0 on z-scaled
  terms) and not searched; k = 20 and smin = 1.5 were picked from the x1b grid (k 10/20/30, smin 1.0/1.5) with
  differences of +-0.1, so there is some in-sample selection in those two knobs. The family fold-OOS over the full
  54-variant grid (1.64 LO / 1.49 L/S on 400 names, 1.78 / 1.48 on 150) is the number that includes that selection.
* Exploration (log*.txt) ran on rlib's v1 caches (targets not split-adjusted across the day boundary); final.py and the
  table above use the v2 caches. On the overlapping books the difference is <= 0.05 Sharpe.
* Second-half decay is real for the L/S books (3.1 / 0.6 and 3.0 / 1.2) and milder for the LO (2.9 / 1.4).

## final.py
`nohup python3 agents/xsrule/final.py > agents/xsrule/final.log 2>&1 &` regenerates exactly the three kept candidates via
rlib.save_cand("xsrule", ...) from rlib.universe(1500) (1500 names), honouring STRAT_END through rlib.DEV_END, and prints
the 1.5 bps repricing, the fold-OOS of the dict and of each candidate. Runtime: ~3 min when rlib's v2 caches and the
lane's own per-ticker extra cache (`agents/xsrule/xcache_root/xcache`, ~45 MB, gitignored; override with XS_SCRATCH) are warm; ~35-45 min from cold (rlib.ticker_frame ~2 min per 400 names, ticker_extra similar). Peak memory
~4 GB (2.5M-row frame); no intermediate frames are written. A per-ticker cache file that is being written by another
process at the same moment is skipped with a "skip" line (one name of 1500 in one run; harmless).
